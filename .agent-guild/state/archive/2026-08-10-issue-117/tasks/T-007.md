## Spec excerpt

Every other authoring task has landed. This one writes the commit messages and takes the final pass over the job's prose as a whole.

**Write `.agent-guild/state/commit-message.md`.** Two messages, each under its own heading, because this job produces two commits in two repositories:

1. **This repo** (`agent-guild`): the `job` field on the vendor-call ledger — schema, derivation in `ledger-append.py`, tests, the retrospective's archive step, docs, and working memory.
2. **The skills repo** (`~/repos/skills`): the backfill of 18 rows in `.agent-guild/state/archive/2026-08-08-issue-27/log/vendor-calls.jsonl`, adding `job` to each.

Both in conventional-commit style with a scope, e.g. `fix(ledger): ...`. The subject says what changed; the body says **why it mattered**, which here is that a task id is unique inside a job and meaningless across jobs, so two runs' rows collided in one file with only a timestamp separating them, and one of those timestamps was wrong. Just long enough to cover what's essential.

Two hard rules on those messages. **No hard wrapping**: let the terminal and git's pager wrap at display time, because a hard-wrapped body renders badly in GitHub's UI and in IDEs that show full width. **No `Co-Authored-By` trailer** or any other coauthored attribution, on either message.

**Then read the job's prose as a whole** and fix anything that reads as generated rather than written: the `job` description in `.agent-guild/schemas/vendor-call.schema.json`, `ledger-append.py`'s module docstring, step 3 of `guild-core/workflows/retrospective/SKILL.md`, and the addition to `docs/vendor-ledger.md`. You have the Skill tool; invoke `humanizer` and follow its audit loop.

**Three house overrides beat the skill wherever they conflict.** The first two, plus the no-hard-wrap rule above, are this project's own standard at `_working-memory/conventions.md:65`; the third is this job's. Your checker has been given the same three:

- **Em dashes stay** where they beat a comma or a colon, chained directly to the text on both sides with no surrounding spaces—like this. The skill's §14 bans them outright; here it does not apply.
- **Headings stay Title Case.** The skill's §17 says lowercase; ignore it.
- **A list of exactly three is fine** when three is the true count. §10 is about padding to three, not about three being wrong.

**Two things the voice pass must not break**, both of which your own checker re-confirms:

- Step 3 of the retrospective skill has to keep naming `log/` **inside an enumeration** of what moves, and keep its one sentence of reason. The humanizer pushes against enumerations and against sentences that explain why something matters; both instincts are wrong here, because that list is the instruction and the reason is why anyone follows it. Improve how it reads; do not dissolve it into prose.
- The six files T-006 wrote have to keep every fact they carry, including the three findings in `_working-memory/openQuestions.md` and their counts.

You may edit the wording of anything named above, including the retrospective step that T-005 wrote. That is deliberate and does not collide with T-005: its clause is whether the step *instructs* correctly, yours is how it *reads*. Do not change what any of these files says, only how it says it, and do not touch behavior in the script or the schema.

**If you find a fact missing rather than badly worded, report it — do not write it.** You hold no authoring license over the three working-memory files, and a gap that arrived before your pass belongs to the task that wrote it. State it plainly in the message you finish with, so the orchestrator sees it: notes are off-limits to the orchestrator by design, so a report that lives only there reaches nobody. Your checker will fail the clause and name the owning task, and the orchestrator reopens that task rather than sending this one back — you are not expected to clear it.

Do not commit anything. The orchestrator makes both commits after the job ends, using the messages you write here verbatim.

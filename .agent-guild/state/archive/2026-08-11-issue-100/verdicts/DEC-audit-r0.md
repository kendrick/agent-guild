## The live-gate hazard

Preamble rule 3 asks for two things: the worker writes the whole change for its task before finishing, and the orchestrator keeps PAUSED in reach. T-002 carries the worker half correctly and with the reason attached — "these are the live gates this session is running. A half-finished edit can block the orchestrator's own turn, so land the whole change before you finish rather than leaving one gate wired and the other not." The PAUSED half is an orchestrator escape and correctly absent from a worker brief.

The load-bearing protection is the cut itself, and it is the right cut. Both gates ship in one task because a `stop-gate.py` that demands a courier while `dispatch-guard.py` still refuses one on status is an unrecoverable loop that ends at `STALLED.md`. T-002's excerpt states this in its second paragraph and repeats the mechanism in the dispatch-guard section, so a worker tempted to land the stop gate first and "come back for the guard" has been told twice why not.

I traced the two moments the gate could brick the orchestrator's own turn and neither does:

1. **The instant T-002's edits hit disk.** The live session execs `.agent-guild/hooks/stop-gate.py` from source on every Stop. But the orchestrator's turn does not end while a subagent is in flight, so the transient window between the worker's first and last write is not a window in which the gate runs. The worker's own SubagentStop is `subagent-return`'s jurisdiction, and T-002 explicitly protects the `in_subagent()` no-op at `stop-gate.py:94` that keeps a subagent from touching the livelock counter.
2. **The instant T-002's verdict of record lands.** The gate is live by then, so `T-002-sonnet-r0.json` immediately owes a crossing and the stop gate blocks. The orchestrator dispatches the courier; the task is still at `checking`, which was already legal for a courier before this job. The debt discharges when the sibling lands. If T-002's verdict is a FAIL instead, the task moves to `rework` with the debt still standing, and the widening T-002 itself built is what admits the courier there. The loop closes in both directions.

Preamble rule 4's other consequence — verdicts written before the gate existed start owing debts retroactively — is an orchestrator obligation (dispatch a courier after every checker of record from T-001 onward) and correctly lives in the constitution rather than in a task file. Worth confirming that it has actually been honored: if `T-001-sonnet-r0.json` lands without a `-codex` sibling before T-002 completes, the stop gate will block on it the moment T-002 goes live. That is recoverable rather than fatal — `dispatch-guard` by then admits the courier on a `complete` task — but it is a turn spent on paperwork the rule was written to avoid. The auditor stems already on disk are safe: `CON-audit-r0.md` and `CON-audit-r1.md` are `.md` only, and the predicate matches `T-NNN-` stems.

Nothing in the cut makes the hazard worse than it needs to be. The one cost the ordering does impose is that T-002 switches enforcement on before a single suite case covers it — T-003 comes after — so for the duration of T-003's build, the only thing that has ever exercised the live gate is T-002's checker driving it against scratch fixtures. That is unavoidable: the cases cannot pass before the wiring exists. It does mean the T-003 finding above is worth more than it looks, since the suite is the only durable record that any of this was ever verified.

One boundary the checks do not reach, recorded so it is not mistaken for an oversight. C-4's text says the widening applies "whatever that task's status," but all four of its confirmations exercise `complete`. A worker implementing `status in ("checking", "complete")` passes every confirmation and then refuses a courier on a `rework` task — which, per moment 2 above, is a state this very job can reach. T-002's excerpt does say "whatever its status," and C-4's rubric orders a source read, so a checker doing its job catches it. Flagging it as the mutation most likely to survive the check rather than as a defect in the cut.

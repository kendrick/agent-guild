## Spec excerpt

T-001, T-002, and T-003 all edit build inputs: `.agent-guild/hooks/` and
`.agent-guild/CLAUDE.md` are copied into both packaged templates by
`scripts/build-plugin.py`. The committed `plugin/`, `plugins/agent-guild/`, and
`.claude/` trees are generated and drift the moment a source file changes, and
`build-plugin.py --check` fails the build on that drift.

Run `python3 scripts/build-plugin.py`, then prove the whole surface is green.

This task is terminal by construction: it must run after every task that edits a
build input, or a later edit leaves the trees stale while every verdict reads
green. #117 lost an audit round to exactly that ordering mistake.

Beyond the suites, prove each of these:

- Nothing under `.agent-guild/state/archive/` changed, and every archived verdict
  still passes `validate-verdict.py`. The archive is #34's evidence corpus,
  including the two crossings this whole issue was filed over, and it is the one
  part of `state/` that git tracks (`.gitignore:6` un-ignores it). No migration,
  no backfill, no authorization record is added to a historical crossing.
- `scripts/classify-crossings.py` still runs. It reads the verdict-directory
  shape to build #34's corpus and has no test suite of its own, so a change to
  what lives in `state/verdicts/` can break it silently.

Never hand-edit a generated tree. If `--check` reports drift, the fix is to
correct the source under `.agent-guild/` or `guild-core/` and rebuild.


**Commit your own work.** C-9 checks a commit subject, and a working-tree diff
has none. When your artifacts are in place and your own verification passes,
commit them with a conventional-commit subject carrying a type and a scope, and
a body saying why the change was made rather than what it changed. Do not push;
the orchestrator owns that.

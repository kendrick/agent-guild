## Spec excerpt

`dispatch-guard.py:43` stamps `dispatches.log` with
`time.strftime("%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%S")`: local time, no offset, no marker. Verdicts
and ledger rows stamp UTC with a trailing `Z` via `utc_now()`. On the machine
that produced the #100 archive those two clocks are exactly five hours apart, and
nothing in either file records that they differ, so comparing a dispatch row
against a verdict timestamp is silently wrong by the offset. Reading them as one
clock makes a forged verdict look like it preceded the dispatch that would have
authorized it.

Change `_log` to write UTC with a trailing `Z`, matching `utc_now()`'s format
exactly (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`, whole seconds).

Scope, precisely:

- `_log` only. `_log_gate_gap` at `:51` writes a different file
  (`gate-gaps.log`) and is out of scope for this task; leave it alone.
- `.agent-guild/hooks/` is stdlib-only and must not import from
  `.agent-guild/scripts/`, so use `datetime` directly rather than reaching for
  `_courier_lib.utc_now`.
- Rows already written under `.agent-guild/state/archive/` are local-time and
  stay that way. Do not migrate them; C-6 forbids touching the archive.

Verify with `python3 .agent-guild/state/check-141-conformance.py dispatch-clock`,
which calls `_log` directly and reads the row back. It exits 1 today and prints
the offending stamp.


**Commit your own work.** C-9 checks a commit subject, and a working-tree diff
has none. When your artifacts are in place and your own verification passes,
commit them with a conventional-commit subject carrying a type and a scope, and
a body saying why the change was made rather than what it changed. Do not push;
the orchestrator owns that.

**The disagreement, on C-9.** Both read the same three-line comment. The checker
judged that it carries a why unrecoverable from the code: these rows are compared
against verdict and ledger stamps, and a local stamp is *silently* wrong by the
machine's offset. The courier agreed that far and said it stops one step short of
the consequence that motivated the work, namely that mixed clocks let a forged
verdict appear to precede the dispatch that would have authorized it. Recorded as
`substantive`: the courier had the evidence and reasoned from it, rather than
declining to certify something it was never shown.

**A confound worth more than the finding.** The brief was `attack`-framed and it
contained that #100 consequence in its own background section, because the far
side cannot read the repo and needed it. So the courier was handed a richer
explanation than the artifact contains and then asked to find fault with the
artifact. A disagreement produced that way is not clean evidence about model
family, and the schema has no field that captures it. Future crossings on prose

of the two supplied it. This is the first case where the framing itself is the
likeliest cause of the split.
